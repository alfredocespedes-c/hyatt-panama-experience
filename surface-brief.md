# Surface brief — QR immersion and form

## Scope and visitor mode

Primary artifact: mobile QR experience. Mode: Experience first, then Configure.

## Audience, job, action, proof and constraints

Hyatt and PepsiCo attendees on mobile enter a short cinematic journey and submit four contact fields. The proof is continuity: Panama City resolves into the actual Hyatt destination, and the ballroom projection becomes the form. The experience must remain usable without video, must load over event connectivity, and may ship only original or licensed visuals.

## Chosen direction

Restrained architectural elegance. The page behaves like entering a precisely lit building rather than browsing a campaign site. The memorable moment is a rapid approach into the Regency Ballroom followed by a seamless transformation of its projection screen into the first question.

## Direction contract

**THESIS:** Arrival is the interface. Refuse a branded landing hero followed by an unrelated embedded form.

**OWN-WORLD:** Deep mineral blue-black, warm limestone, brushed silver and Pepsi blue used as controlled light. Thin architectural rules, rectilinear framing, restrained radii and spacious typography. Controls read as projection architecture, not rounded SaaS cards.

**STORY:** The attendee taps once, travels from Panama to the hotel and into the Regency Ballroom, then answers one field per scene. A final confirmation closes the experience and explains that the response was saved.

**FIRST VIEWPORT:** Full-bleed moving architectural field with a quiet event lockup and one unmistakable “Begin the experience” control near the lower third. No menu, card grid or introductory paragraph. A visible skip route sits outside the main action.

**FORM:** User-pinned architectural elegance (seed: user-pinned; concept roll not applicable); code-led with one persistent procedural WebGL scene. Signature interaction: a continuous camera travels across a conceptual white architectural masterplan, approaches a two-tower hotel massing, resolves inside the ballroom, and lets the projection rectangle expand into the form without an obvious page cut. CSS/SVG remains the lightweight fallback.

**FINISH:** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
